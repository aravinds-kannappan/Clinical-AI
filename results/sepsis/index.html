Research prototype · Public data and simulation

Can consent and human review constrain clinical AI without overwhelming care?

We evaluate information flow, prediction error and review capacity separately. EHR modeling is the main empirical study; public skeletons are a secondary check.

No clinical validation or patient-benefit claim. Primary EHR results use 6,000 public sepsis-benchmark patients. The earlier 100-patient MIMIC demo is retained as a separate limited check. Queue behavior is simulated. Structural proofs cover the implemented graph, not an unmodeled clinical system.

H1 · Structure reveals risk

Exact path enumeration exposes review bypasses and cached-data routes. Degree and betweenness guide removal, delay and corruption tests.

H2 · Constraints preserve utility

The consent mask blocks storage while preserving an ephemeral nurse alert. Useful predictive performance and feasible queues must be established separately.

H3 · Structure must earn its place

Compare graph models against summary, topology-free and rewired alternatives. Tiny participant counts limit conclusions about movement-model superiority.

What the study found

Conclusion: a complete affirmative answer was not established. Consent and mandatory-review constraints are implementable and auditable in this model. Their coexistence with clinically useful predictions and effective human oversight remains unproven; clinician review quality and patient outcomes were not measured.

In the prespecified seed-11 hospital-A test, the all-patient rule had 4.68% average missed-window loss, but 50.14% missed-window loss among positive patients. Calibrating on positive patients reduced conditional missed loss to 4.13%, while alerting on 92.69% of hourly observations.

For the same positive-patient rule, conditional missed loss rose to 26.25% in held-out hospital B. There is no asserted risk guarantee under that hospital shift. The question is therefore both which risk is controlled and whether the resulting review demand can be served.

These are benchmark point estimates for one prespecified seed; the report includes all three seeds. Read the full results and limitations.

What the mathematics guarantees—and what it does not

Consent: A_allowed(i,p) = A ⊙ M(i,p), independently for patient and purpose. Provenance-aware withdrawal invalidates future use through cached derivatives.

Review: remove mandatory review nodes and enumerate remaining sensitive-source-to-action paths. No surviving path establishes the structural constraint; availability is checked separately.

Risk: for the selected patient population (all patients or positive patients), select the smallest lambda satisfying (n R_hat(lambda)+1)/(n+1) ≤ .05. Loss is each patient's missed positive-window fraction, zero for patients with no positives. B=1.

Assumptions: frozen model; exchangeable patient loss functions (conditional on positive-patient status for the positive-patient rule); bounded, non-increasing, right-continuous loss; terminal all-alert rule with zero loss. The guarantee averages over calibration and a future patient. It is not per patient, subgroup, or shifted site. Alert dropping after calibration changes the policy.

Capacity: expected reviews ≤ K does not bound every realized queue. Service time, burstiness, backlog, overflow and completed reviews are reported separately.

Study design and evidence limits
  • Three fixed seeds; disjoint training, calibration and test patients; preprocessing fitted on training patients only.
  • Sepsis endpoint: the provided hourly early-warning target, already shifted six hours early; it also includes later positive hours. Features use current/past rows only. Store timestamps are unavailable.
  • Hospital A is split 60/20/20% by patient; all sampled hospital B patients form an external-site shift test. Absolute dates are unavailable, so no calendar holdout is claimed.
  • 500 patient-bootstrap replicates for prespecified seed-11 intervals and paired effects. Whiskers in discrimination views are patient-bootstrap confidence intervals; risk/workload views show point estimates only.
  • UP-Fall derivative has five participants. Repetition-order checks are not calendar validation; one-patient calibration yields an all-alert fallback.
  • Provider/order edges are an ordering proxy, not note-access logs or evidence of effective teamwork.
  • Blocked graph reachability is not a reconstruction or re-identification privacy guarantee. Raw-video privacy attacks were deferred under the revised scope.
