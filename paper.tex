\begin{abstract}
Can a sepsis prediction system retain useful performance when patients restrict its inputs? We present a reproducible benchmark evaluation on all 40,336 public PhysioNet 2019 patients, comparing three supervised models across three split seeds, two hospitals and eight simulated permission conditions. Within each comparison, patients, labels, fitted predictors and alert thresholds remain fixed; only permitted information and derived state change. Paired whole-patient resampling measures differences in discrimination, incident misses and review burden. At a 30\% independent denial probability for each physiological feature group, mean AUROC falls by 0.019--0.051 across model--hospital combinations. Effects depend strongly on the restricted source. At hospital B, removing laboratory inputs changes GRU AUROC from 0.752 to 0.745, yet increases missed-incident risk by 11.2 percentage points at the original hourly alert threshold while reducing reviews by 1.42 per patient. Thus similar ranking performance can conceal a substantial change in alert behavior. We formalize paired loss changes and give a bounded-loss preservation criterion with an explicit proof based on established concentration methods. Joint policy analyses evaluate alert timing, review capacity and modeled oversight alongside the paired input comparison. Hourly trajectories and simulated withdrawal at hour 12 make the monitoring-time scope explicit. The contribution is an evaluation protocol and measured failure analysis, not a new predictor or a determination of ethical acceptability. Restrictions act at inference time on models trained with full public inputs; real consent, clinical non-inferiority and patient benefit remain unmeasured.
\end{abstract}
